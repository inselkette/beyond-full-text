# Beyond Full Text: AI-Assisted Discovery of Public Sector Documents

## The Theme and Question

Libraries collect and archive documents that cannot always be made publicly available due to copyright restrictions. While these documents are preserved and accessible internally, their content often remains inaccessible to the wider public.

**Research question:** Can AI-generated summaries provide a copyright-conscious way to make the content of archived documents publicly accessible without republishing the original full texts?

## The Material

The project will use approximately 30–50 publicly available government documents, primarily PDFs and Word files. Documents will be selected based on their relevance, diversity, and suitable conditions of use.

## Why AI?

Building on an existing Python-based document harvesting workflow, the project will develop a pipeline that extracts text, generates summaries using large language models (LLMs), and presents the results through a standalone web interface.

At least two LLMs and different prompting strategies will be evaluated for accuracy, completeness, and potential reproduction of copyrighted passages. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) may support source-grounded analysis across documents.

Optional extensions include visualizing thematic relationships and recommending relevant literature from library catalogues.

## Who Is It For?

Researchers, students, librarians, and the general public seeking accessible information about government publications. The prototype could eventually be adapted for use in libraries.

## Your Team

An individual project combining professional experience in library information systems and metadata management with Python programming and Digital Humanities methods.

## Concerns

The main challenge is establishing whether AI-generated summaries can be legally processed and published. Public accessibility does not automatically permit unrestricted reuse.

Other concerns include factual accuracy, hallucinations, and transparency. The prototype will use documents with suitable permissions, provide links to original sources, and require human review before publication.
